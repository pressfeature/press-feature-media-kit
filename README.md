# PressFeature Media Kit: What a Sponsored Article Includes, What It Costs, and How to Book One

## What a Placement Includes

A sponsored article placement is a bundle, and careful buyers learn exactly what sits inside the bundle before they compare prices.

**The article as approved.** The buyer approves the copy before anything is published. That approval covers the topic, the claims the article makes, the quotes it carries, and the overall framing. Nothing is rewritten after approval without the buyer's sign-off, so the published piece says what the business meant it to say.

**The named outlet.** The placement names the specific news outlet where the article will appear, and it names it before payment. Not a category such as "a major US news site," and not a promise of something comparable. The outlet's name is what the buyer is truly purchasing: its audience, its reputation, and its domain.

**The agreed link policy.** Before money changes hands, both sides confirm in writing what the article may link to: which pages, how many links, and whether those links pass search value. Search value lives or dies on these details, so a placement without a written link policy is not a finished offer.

**The live link.** The deliverable is a URL on the outlet's own domain, delivered to the buyer once the article is published. Screenshots and verbal promises are not substitutes. The live link is what goes on the press page, into the pitch deck, and in front of customers.

**The "As Featured On" value afterward.** Once the article is live, the buyer can truthfully say it was featured on the outlet. That badge value compounds over time. It appears on the homepage, in sales materials, in investor updates, and anywhere trust must be established quickly. A single placement keeps paying in credibility long after publication day, which is why buyers should treat the price as the purchase of an asset rather than the rental of attention.

## The Four Cost Factors

No two placements cost the same, and the differences come down to four factors.

**1. Audience size.** An outlet read by millions commands more than one read by thousands, because the buyer pays for exposure as well as credibility. National outlets with broad readership sit at the top of the range, while city and niche outlets cost less and often convert better for local businesses. The right question is never size in isolation but size among the people the buyer needs to reach.

**2. Site authority.** Search engines trust some domains more than others, and part of that trust transfers through links. An article on a long-established outlet with strong search visibility carries more weight than the same article on a new or obscure site. Buyers who care about rankings should weigh authority as heavily as raw traffic.

**3. Speed.** Turnaround starts at 24 hours on outlets that move fast, and faster publication costs more because it consumes priority in an editorial queue. A business with a launch date gladly pays for certainty, while a business with no deadline can save money by accepting a longer window. Either way, the timeline belongs in the written approval before payment.

**4. What is included.** Some placements cover professional writing and a round of revisions; others expect the buyer to supply finished copy. Some include multiple links or prominent placement; others include a single link in the body text. Two quotes for "one article" can describe very different bundles, so buyers should compare inclusions line by line rather than prices alone.

## Why Flat Price Lists Mislead

A single flat price list for every outlet looks transparent, but it hides more than it reveals. Outlets differ in audience, authority, speed, and inclusions, so one price cannot fit them all without distorting something. When a seller publishes one price for everything, buyers should ask what is being averaged away. Usually it is the link policy, the timeline, or the quality of the outlet itself.

Worse, a flat list invites the bait and switch. A buyer attracted by the headline price may discover at checkout that the outlets it actually wants cost extra, or that the advertised price covers a link-free mention rather than a full article. Serious sellers price per placement, in writing, with the outlet named. Anything less deserves skepticism.
